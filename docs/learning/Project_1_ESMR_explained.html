Regional noise reduction of NIMBUS 5 ESMR sea ice concentration

One question: how much does an ERA5 + radiative-transfer correction reduce the noise in sea ice concentration (SIC) over open water?

1 · The project in 30 seconds

Open water should give SIC = 0 %. Weather (water vapour, wind, temperature) shifts the measured brightness temperature TB, so open water shows fake ice of a few percent. You remove that with a model and ERA5, then measure how much the scatter dropped.

2 · The instrument: ESMR on NIMBUS 5

  • One channel: 19.35 GHz, horizontal polarisation. Dec 1972 – May 1977.
  • Cross-track scanner (not conical!): 78 scan positions, incidence angle 0 → ~64° each side, swath ~3100 km.
  • Footprint ~25 km at nadir → ~160 × 45 km at the swath edge. Outer 4 positions (57–64°) are dropped in Kolbe et al.
  • Advantage: no pole hole. Disadvantage: the angle changes across the swath, and so does the physics.

🟢 Kolbe_etal_essd-16-1247-2024.pdf, p. 1248 (Sect. 2), p. 1249–1250 · sea_ice_lecture1.pdf, p. 5

3 · Why water and ice look different at 19 GHz H

  • Water: high permittivity → strong Fresnel reflection → low emissivity, especially at H-pol.
  • Wind roughens the surface → emissivity rises → water looks warmer → fake ice.
  • Water vapour and clouds emit → TB rises over water (dark background), barely over ice.

🟢 sea_ice_lecture1.pdf, p. 4, 48, 69–70 · Tie-point levels: sea_ice_lecture2.pdf, p. 32 (water ~140 K, ice ~220–230 K) · Kolbe p. 1251, Table 1

4 · The forward model (RTM)

Given the weather and surface, what TB does the satellite see?

T_B = RTM(V, W, L, Ts, Ti, c_ice, θ) T_B = T_BU + τ·[ (1−c)·E_w·Ts + (1−c)(1−E_w)(Ω·T_BD + τ·T_BC) + c ·E_i·Ti + c (1−E_i)(T_BD + τ·T_BC) ]
TBU, TBDup/down-welling atmosphere emission (V, L, Ts)
τatmospheric transmissivity; path ∝ 1/cos θ → bigger effect at high θ
Ew = E0 + EWcalm-sea Fresnel emissivity + wind part EW = W(0.0094θ + 0.3)/Ts
Ei(θ)first-year ice emissivity, look-up table
Ωroughness reduces reflected sky
TBCcosmic background 2.7 K

You get it ready-made: esmr_rtm.py → esmr(V,W,L,Ts,Ti,c_ice,theta). One pixel per call (scalar), so loop or vectorise.

🟢 Kolbe p. 1250, eq. 5–12 · sea_ice_lecture1.pdf, p. 72–76 · sea_ice_lecture2.pdf, p. 30 · ESMR_proj/esmr_rtm.py

5 · One-channel SIC with tie points

c_ice = (T_B − T_p,water) / (T_p,ice − T_p,water)

Tie points = typical TB of 100 % water and 100 % ice. In Kolbe et al. they are dynamic: picked daily from the data (criteria: ERA5 SIC, SST > 278 K for water, latitude, TB range), then a 15-day running mean.

With one channel you cannot separate ice type and temperature (lecture: “one channel algorithms are sensitive to target temperature and sea ice type”). Over open water this does not bite, which is why your project looks there.

🟢 Kolbe p. 1251–1253, eq. 16, Table 2 · sea_ice_lecture2.pdf, p. 11–12, 32–35

6 · The core idea: correction by difference

T_B,corrected = T_B,measured + ΔT_B ΔT_B = RTM( mean V, mean W, mean L, mean Ts, mean Ti, c, θ ) ← reference weather − RTM( ERA5 V, ERA5 W, mean L, ERA5 Ts, ERA5 Ti, c, θ ) ← actual weather Ti = 0.4·T2m + 0.6·272
  • You push every pixel to the same reference weather (the tie-point mean). What is left should be ice only.
  • Only the difference of two model runs is used → constant model bias cancels.
  • L (cloud liquid) is not corrected: mean L goes into both runs.
  • c is needed inside the RTM → 2 steps: SIC from uncorrected TB (clip 0–1, < 0.15 → 0), correct, recompute SIC.

🟢 Kolbe p. 1251–1253, eq. 13–15 and steps 1–2 · sea_ice_lecture2.pdf, p. 27, 31

Check: why not just compute TB with ERA5 and subtract it? Because the RTM has an absolute bias of a few K (~2 K vs other models, Kolbe p. 1251). Subtracting the full model would put that bias straight into SIC. The difference of two runs with the same model cancels it.

9 · Traps

1 · Scaling. Brightness_temperature, Latitude, Longitude are stored ×10 (K×10, °×10). Divide first. 🟢 Kolbe Table A1
2 · Angle. NASA's correction removed the natural angle dependence of TB; Kolbe et al. could not undo it. The RTM does vary with θ. Keep this in mind when you interpret task 3. 🟢 Kolbe p. 1249, p. 1258 (“possible improvement: angle-dependent tie points”)
3 · Incidence angle is not in the file. Map scan position (1–78) → θ with spos2ia.txt; negative = left side, symmetric. RTM needs |θ|, and the ice table only goes to 60°. 🟢 read_me.docx · esmr_rtm.py
4 · Wind is a vector. W = √(u10² + v10²). V = tcwv. L is not directly in the list (tcw = total column water). 🟢 Kolbe Table A1 · ⚠️ how to get L is not stated: ask Rasmus Tonboe, or use mean L (it is not corrected anyway)
5 · Open water selection. Use the water criteria (ERA5 SIC = 0, 5×5 mean < 0.01, SST > 278 K, TB 90–180 K, latitude band). Otherwise ice edge pixels inflate your STD. 🟢 Kolbe Table 2
6 · Don't clip SIC to 0–1 for the statistics. Mean ≈ 0 over water only works if negative values stay. 🟡 my advice; check with your supervisor.

11 · Explain it back (say it aloud)

Why does wind create fake ice over open water?Wind roughens the surface → emissivity at H-pol goes up → TB rises toward the ice tie point → SIC > 0. Effect grows with θ (0.0094θ + 0.3 K per m/s).
Why is the correction a difference of two RTM runs?Model bias cancels; you only move each pixel from its actual weather to the reference weather.
Why can corrected STD never reach 0?Instrument noise (~3 K ≈ 3–4 % SIC), ERA5 errors, and processes the RTM doesn't model.
Why is the correction better over water than over ice?Over ice the main noise is in snow/ice (layers, grain size, melt), which the RTM doesn't describe; atmosphere is only ~⅓ of the noise there. (Kolbe p. 1253, 1257)
