The ESMR paper as your project map

The whole paper in one sentence: simulate the weather effect on every ESMR pixel twice with the RTM (average weather minus actual ERA5 weather), add the difference to the measured TB, and the open-water noise in the sea ice concentration shrinks. Your project measures how much it shrinks, which ERA5 inputs do the work, and at which incidence angles.

Mini-Check

You need the wind speed W for the RTM. What do you do?

ERA5 stores the 10 m wind as two components. The roughness depends on the speed, the length √(u²+v²). A sum can cancel to zero in a storm with opposite signs, and u10 alone misses half the wind.

Mini-Check

Why does Eq. 4 look at seven pixels in a row instead of two?

TB is stored to 0.1 K, so two neighbours can be equal by chance over calm water. Seven identical values in a row (at least 175 km) are a stuck reading. The window runs along track, not across the swath.

Method⏱ 10 minPages 5–7

5 The correction moves every pixel to "average weather": RTM with the means minus RTM with the actual ERA5 values

The one thing

Eq. 14 runs the RTM twice for the same pixel: once with the tie-point mean weather, once with the pixel's own ERA5 weather. The difference is added to the measured TB (Eq. 13). Because only a difference is used, a constant model bias drops out. L stays at its mean in both runs, so clouds are not corrected.

\[ T_{B,\mathrm{corr}}=T_{B,\mathrm{meas}}+\underbrace{\mathrm{RTM}(\bar V,\bar W,\bar L,\bar T_s,\bar T_i,c,\theta)-\mathrm{RTM}(V,W,\bar L,T_s,T_i,c,\theta)}_{\Delta T_{B,\mathrm{simulated}}} \]

Why must open-water tie-point pixels have SST above 278 K?

Method⏱ 9 minPages 7–8

7 SIC needs the RTM and the RTM needs SIC: one iteration, and the open-water filter hides the noise you want to measure

The one thing

Step 1: SIC from uncorrected TB (Eq. 16), clipped to [0, 1], below 15 % set to 0. Step 2: use that c in the RTM, correct, compute SIC again. For your open-water statistics keep the raw, unclipped SIC: clipping turns a zero-mean noise into a positive bias and a too small std.

\[ c_{ice}=\frac{T_{B}-T_{p,\mathrm{water}}}{T_{p,\mathrm{ice}}-T_{p,\mathrm{water}}} \]

Model: open-water pixels with true SIC = 0 and Gaussian noise σ (in % SIC). Switch to "clip + 15 % filter": everything below 15 % piles up at 0, the mean jumps above 0 and the std shrinks although the data are just as noisy.

Uncertainty (Eq. 17–19), only if you have time

Per pixel: algorithm part from the tie-point std, weighted by (1 − c) and c, plus resampling part (max − min in 3 × 3). Over open water the algorithm part is δTp,water / (Tp,ice − Tp,water). Instrument noise is 3 K, about 3 % SIC.

Mini-Check

Your open-water SIC mean is +4 % after correction. What do you check first?

Clipping removes the negative half of the noise, so the mean goes positive by itself (widget). A constant RTM bias cancels in ΔTB. A mean offset can also come from tie points computed on other pixels, but clipping is the cheap thing to rule out first.

Exam⏱ 3 min

Traps that are easy to fall into

The correction subtracts the RTM-simulated TB from the measured one.

It adds a difference of two RTM runs (mean minus actual). The absolute RTM value never enters, so its bias cancels.

Open-water SIC statistics are computed on the final ice_conc.

Use raw, unclipped SIC. Clipping and the 15 % filter create a positive mean and a too small std.

Cloud liquid water is corrected like vapour and wind.

L is set to its mean in both runs (p. 5), so clouds stay as noise. ERA5 in the file has no L.

Ti comes from the ERA5 ice temperature istl1.

Ti = 0.4·t2m + 0.6·272 K (Eq. 15). Over open water it plays no role anyway.

ESMR TB naturally drop with incidence angle, like any H-pol radiometer.

NASA's lobe correction also flattened the angular dependence (p. 3). The data barely vary with θ, the RTM does.

Warmer sea surface means higher TB.

At 19 GHz H the emissivity drops with temperature (permittivity), so TB changes little and even falls slightly. Ts is a weak input.

Scan position 1 is near nadir.

Position 1 is −64°, 39/40 are ±0.76°, 78 is +64°. There is no nadir pixel.

Self-test⏱ 10 min

Self-test: think first, then reveal

Explain in three sentences why the correction uses the difference of two RTM runs.

The RTM and ERA5 have biases. The difference RTM(mean) − RTM(actual) keeps only the change caused by this pixel's weather anomaly. Constant biases appear in both runs and cancel (p. 5).

What would you draw on the board to explain the correction?

A ΔTB-vs-θ sketch like Fig. 4: zero line, a curve that fans out towards the edges, positive for a wet day, negative for a dry one. Next to it the chain: ERA5 → RTM twice → difference → add to TB → SIC with tie points.

Explain in three sentences why the std is largest at the swath edges.

The slant path grows as 1/cos θ, so the atmosphere contributes more. The H-pol water emissivity falls with θ, so more sky is reflected. The edge footprints are also larger and noisier (p. 3).

How do you select open-water pixels for task 1?

Table 2 ocean column: ERA5 siconc = 0, 5 × 5 box mean below 1 %, SST above 278 K, TB 90–180 K, latitude above 32° N (Arctic) or below 48° S (Antarctic).

How would you run the exclusion test in code?

Repeat the correction with one variable replaced by its mean in the "actual" run, e.g. V → V̄. Compare the open-water SIC std with the full correction. The larger the increase, the more that variable matters.

Why are clouds not corrected?

ERA5 in the file has no cloud liquid water L, and the paper inputs the mean L to both runs (p. 5). So cloud variability stays in the corrected TB as noise.

Why does the paper iterate the SIC once?

The RTM needs c as input but c is the output. Step 1 gives c from uncorrected data, step 2 corrects with that c and recomputes. Further iterations change little (p. 8).

Why should your open-water statistics not use the 15 % filter?

The filter sets small values to 0 and clipping removes negative values. Both remove noise you want to measure: the mean moves above 0 and the std shrinks artificially.

Which ERA5 variable do you expect to matter most over open water, and why?

Water vapour V, then wind W. V raises emission and reflected sky radiation, W raises the surface emissivity. Both grow with θ. Ts has a small effect at 19 GHz H.

What does a dip in the water tie points like in mid-1976 mean?

An instrument calibration problem, not real ocean change (Fig. 2, p. 12). The dynamical tie points follow it, so the SIC stays calibrated. The 15-day window keeps it visible.

Explain in three sentences why the paper's corrected std is still 10–14 K.

Instrument noise (3 K) cannot be corrected. Clouds are not corrected. ERA5 misses part of the real weather, and NASA's angular correction does not match the RTM's θ dependence.
