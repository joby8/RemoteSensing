## Figure details (Fig. 4-19a, Feb 1973)
- **Projection:** polar stereographic, 30 x 30 km map elements, centred on the North Pole (pole at about x = 768, y = 787 in `fig_feb1973.jpg`; the grey map frame is about x = 28..1500, y = 28..1540).
- **Orientation (differs from SP-459!):** the **vertical and horizontal lines are the 45 deg meridians in between**; the labelled meridians are the **diagonals**: **180 deg upper left, 90 deg E upper right, 0 deg lower right, 90 deg W lower left** (270 deg E in Fig. B-1a). So 135 deg E points up and **45 deg W points down** (Greenland at the bottom, Bering Strait/Kamchatka at the upper left, Scandinavia/Novaya Zemlya at the right). The map is rotated by 45 deg compared with the usual "0 deg down" view.
- **Graticule:** thin black lines. Meridians every 45 deg (4 lines through the pole). Parallels as circles about every 5 deg from 85 deg N to 50 deg N (best seen in the clean B/W map B-1a; 8 circles, the outermost, labelled "50 deg N", touches the panel edge, radius about half the panel width). Check the radii with r ~ tan((90 - lat)/2): 85 deg ~ 0.12 r(50), 80 deg ~ 0.23, 70 deg ~ 0.48 (should be fitted the same way as in c1_sp459_compare.m).
- **Land:** dark grey-violet, all land masses and also the data-less area (no ice information on land), coastlines and political boundaries as thin black lines. **Open water:** light steel blue (lowest legend class, < 14 %). Panel text "February 1973" at the upper right, the labels 180 deg / 90 deg E / 90 deg W / 50 deg N are printed into the map. The pole is covered by data in the colour map (small pole hole not visible).
- **Legend (nomogram, `legend.jpg`):** two-dimensional. **Rows = ice concentration, columns = multiyear fraction F_MY (0 ... 1)**; diagonal lines show how a colour shifts with F_MY. The colour classes are the **same 8 %-step classes as in SP-459**. Left scale (F_MY = 0, first-year ice, to be used when no multiyear ice): 14, 20, 28, 36, 44, 52, 60, 68, 76, 84, 92, 100 % (class boundaries) - bottom to top: grey-blue (14-20), light blue, medium blue, dark blue (20-36), green classes (36-52), olive/brown (52-60), orange (60-68), red-brown (68-84), dark brown/black (76-92 / 92-100), maroon, magenta-purple (84-92), purple-violet (top) = **100 % (WET)**. Right scale (F_MY = 1, all multiyear): 14, 20, 28, 36, 44, 52, 60, 68, 76, 84, 92, 100 %, shifted (C_MY = 1.283 C_FY). Colour -> concentration is unique only if F_MY is known; without information, one colour gives a range, e.g. the 2nd orange level = 52 % (FY) to 68 % (MY). Ice edge = 14 % contour (colour break between the two lightest blues).
- Colours are dark overall (scan is dark, strong violet cast): nearest-class matching, as for SP-459, needs the swatch colours picked from `legend.jpg` (the legend swatches are inside the same scan, so colour cast is the same).
- **B/W map (Fig. B-1a, `figB1a_feb1973_bw.jpg`):** same orientation/graticule (labels 180 deg E upper left, 90 deg E upper right, 0 deg E lower right, 270 deg E lower left, 50 deg N), **dots = ice (>= 14 %)**, white = water; land shown only as black coastlines. Cleanest source for the ice edge.

## Numbers
- **Algorithm (Sect. 3.5-3.6, p. 95-98):** the Antarctic method of SP-459, with: open-water tie point **T_O = 138.3 K** (Arctic, average over ice-free ocean; SP-459 used 135 K; the 3.3 K difference attributed to atmosphere/surface effects), first-year ice emissivity **e_i = 0.92**, multiyear ice **e_MY = 0.84**. Ice temperature T_i = T_air + f (T_F - T_air), **f = 0.25, T_F = 271.2 K**, T_air = mean monthly climatological air temperature (Crutcher and Meserve 1970, Fig. 2-6, spatially varying) = Eq. 3-16; T_eff (called T_i / T_IF / T_IM) assumed equal for all ice types. Concentration C_i = (T_B - 138.3)/(0.92 T_i - 138.3) (Eq. 3-17); for multiyear ice C_MY = C_i (0.92 T_i - T_O)/(0.84 T_i - T_O) = **1.283 C_i** (for T_i = 248 K, Eq. 3-18). Monthly mean T_B maps (3-day averages averaged over the month, interpolated) -> concentration per 30 km element.
- **Multiyear / central Arctic:** not treated by a separate algorithm. The colour maps use the first-year scale (e_i = 0.92) and the nomogram shows the multiyear ambiguity; in the central Arctic multiyear ice (and summer melt ponds) makes the "concentration" a **pseudo concentration** (pseudo area / pseudo open water). In summer first-year and multiyear ice are indistinguishable. Estimated accuracy: **+-15 % for predominantly first-year ice, +-25 % where the first-year/multiyear mixture is unknown**. Standard deviation of T_air vs. THIR: 5.5 K (Feb).
- **Multiyear correction:** Table 3-1 (Sept 1974/1975: ice area with e = 0.84 is 26 % (Arctic Ocean) / 27 % (north polar) larger than with e = 0.92); Table 3-2 (Jan 1975/1976: total area 11.94 -> 13.14 and 11.90 -> 13.23 x 10^6 km^2 after correction, ~10-11 %, Arctic Ocean 6.29 -> 7.19 / 6.30 -> 7.37). **No such table for Feb 1973.**
- **Feb 1973 extent/area:** **no table; only plots.** Read off the plots (approximate): hemisphere ice extent (>= 15 %), sum of the eight regions, Fig. 5-3 (p. 218): **about 15 x 10^6 km^2** (4-year mean maximum 14.8 x 10^6 in March, min 7.8 x 10^6 in Sept); Arctic Ocean region: extent saturated at the region size (~ 9-11 x 10^6 km^2, whole basin ice-covered in winter in all years), pseudo actual ice area ~ 6.4 x 10^6 km^2 in Feb (Fig. 5-4, p. 219); the January 1975 value in Table 3-2 is 6.29. Plots can be read more precisely from the 300 dpi images (PDF 239/240; Fig. 4-38 PDF 181 for the Arctic Ocean only; Fig. 4-40 PDF 183 year-to-year changes).
- **Feb 1973 specials:** heaviest ice of 1973-76 in the **Sea of Okhotsk** (50 % above the other Februaries; mean concentration close to 80 %, Fig. 4-53) and in the **Greenland Sea** (p. 172, 214); text on the Barents/Kara projection of ice (Fig. 4-19, p. 196).
- **Compare with SP-459:** extent from the 14-15 % class limit, area from sum of concentration x 900 km^2; for the Arctic only extent is directly comparable with ours (area is affected by multiyear ice and melt ponds, and our Arctic ice tie point may not be e_i T_i).
